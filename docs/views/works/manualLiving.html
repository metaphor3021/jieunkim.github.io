newspaper, potassium silicate, reinforced concrete
Manual Living is a series of three domestic structures designed as small interruptions to routine. Each changes how the body balances, how furniture responds, or how people move through space, making familiar moments at home more noticeable.
Project Notes

This project began with my experience of growing up in concrete apartments in South Korea. I spent a lot of time at home, but often could not remember how I had spent that time.

I became interested in how predictable spaces, furniture and repeated routines can make everyday actions increasingly automatic. Manual Living responds through three structures that interrupt sitting, the expected behaviour of furniture and movement through space in different ways.
